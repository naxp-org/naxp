--- source: PostNL ---

A Netherlands postcode comprises four digits then two letters.

The official PostNL format mandates a space as a separator between the digits and the letters. In practice, online forms and backend databases omit the separator, and occasionally older legacy systems or international shippers use a hyphen e.g. 1012-AB, although this is technically incorrect.

As with all postcode systems, a naxp encodes postcodes, but validation is a separate task.

Netherlands postcodes are a case study in how strict original constraints on legal characters can later be relaxed when expansion is required. The 1977 format excluded
(a) the letters F, I, O, Q, U and Y – dropped in 2005, and
(b) zero (0) as a first digit – dropped in 2025, with postcodes 0000 AA to 0999 ZZ allocated for the Caribbean Netherlands.
